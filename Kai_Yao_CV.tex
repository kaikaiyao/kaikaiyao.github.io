\section*{Research Profile}
I am an AI researcher specializing in secure and trustworthy artificial intelligence, with a primary focus on AIGC content provenance, post-deployment model auditing, adversarial robustness, and AI governance. My doctoral research at the University of Edinburgh combines statistical theory, adaptive threat modelling, and large-scale empirical evaluation to establish when passive provenance, watermarking, and output-only auditing remain reliable under realistic editing, silent model substitutions, and adversarial verification interfaces, alongside work on privacy-induced unfairness and AI-mediated education. In addition, my interdisciplinary work in AI for Science applies deep learning and quantitative microscopy to label-free single-cell measurement.

\section*{Research Achievements \& Major Research Projects}

\role{University of Edinburgh}{PhD Researcher, School of Informatics}{Edinburgh, UK}{2023 -- Present}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1.7pt]
  \item \textbf{Output-Only Model Auditing \& Keyed Passive Attribution (\textit{NeurIPS} 2026):} Commercial AI services can silently swap a certified generator for a cheaper or degraded model after deployment, while adaptive attackers can evade public detectors. Developed \textit{FARE} to catch hidden model, checkpoint, quantization, pruning, and step-reduction swaps from outputs alone without accessing weights (92.6\%--99.5\% single-image TPR at 1\% FPR; 92.6\% vs.\ 18.3\% best baseline on Stable Diffusion; 98.8\%--99.5\% small-batch TPR at 0.1\% FPR; 92.4\%--99.4\% under white-box PGD). Complementarily designed \textit{SPRINT}, assigning verifier-held secret pixel targets to attribute six generators at 98.83\% clean accuracy while holding offline adaptive evasion below 1\%.
  \item \textbf{Fundamental Statistical \& Interface Limits of Passive Provenance (\textit{NeurIPS} 2026):} Answered a foundational question—whether image pixels alone can reliably certify origin after everyday editing. Proved a two-barrier minimax framework establishing (i) the image-only total-variation separability ceiling ($\Delta_{s,t}$) beyond which no detector can succeed under editing, and (ii) how score-revealing public APIs leak enough signal for surrogate evasion—showing that low-bandwidth abstention lowers evaluated CLIP attack success from 1.00 to 0.00--0.09.
  \item \textbf{Adversarial Security Benchmarks for Fingerprints \& Local Watermarks (\textit{IEEE SaTML} 2026; \textit{ACM AISec} 2026):} Built a unified white-box and black-box removal/forgery evaluation across 14 fingerprinting methods and 12 GAN/VAE/diffusion generators, revealing that existing fingerprints can be stripped at >80\% white-box and >50\% black-box success (covered by University of Edinburgh News, DIGIT, and Herald Scotland). Co-developed a 55-transformation local-watermark benchmark (4 datasets, 5 methods) separating localization failure from payload failure under cropping, geometric distortion, and generative editing.
  \item \textbf{Trustworthy AI, Privacy Fairness \& AI Governance (\textit{IEEE SaTML} 2025; \textit{AAAI/ACM AIES} 2026 $\times$ 2):} Examined unintended societal harms in deployed AI. Systematized 20 differential-privacy fairness studies into a four-layer causal taxonomy explaining why privacy protection degrades subgroup accuracy unevenly; authored two \textit{AIES} papers defining cognitive stewardship in AI-mediated education alongside an automated audit of 30 university AI policies across four open-weight LLMs.
\end{itemize}

\role{Johns Hopkins University}{Graduate Researcher, Quantitative Biology \& Deep Learning}{Baltimore, MD, USA}{2017 -- 2020}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1.9pt]
  \item \textbf{Label-Free Single-Cell Measurement (\textit{CTRL}, \textit{J.\ Cell Sci.}\ 2020; \textit{J.\ Cell Biol.}\ 2019; \textit{Bio-protocol} 2020):} Measuring live single-cell volume normally requires invasive fluorescent dyes. Paired standard optical microscopy with microfluidic physical ground truth to train U-Net models predicting dynamic cell volume directly from single label-free images (<6\% MAE across biological repeats), supporting biological discovery of YAP/TAZ cell-volume regulation.
  \item \textbf{Cross-Batch Generalization in Live-Cell Imaging (\textit{Scientific Reports}, 2019):} Demonstrated that standard within-flask splits (>95\% accuracy) conceal severe cross-batch shift on unseen culture flasks, and introduced multi-flask mixed training with unsupervised morphological phenotyping to lift unseen-batch accuracy up to 80\%.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Research Agenda}
My ongoing and future research focuses on trustworthy visual content provenance (images and video), connecting theoretical bounds, system-level design, and actionable AI governance:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=3.2pt]
  \item \textbf{Real-World Benchmarking \& Application-Dependent Visual Provenance:} Stress-test deployed industry watermarks (e.g., \mbox{SynthID}) and extend image benchmarks to video (temporal compression, cross-frame edits); design application-dependent provenance schemes for task-specific trade-offs across robustness, imperceptibility, capacity, and false-positive budgets.
  \item \textbf{System-Level Provenance (C2PA + Watermarking + Fingerprinting):} Co-design layered architectures combining C2PA metadata, active watermarking, and passive fingerprinting/auditing (\textit{FARE}, \textit{SPRINT}) to resist metadata stripping and re-encoding.
  \item \textbf{Agentic-AI-Driven Provenance Offense \& Defense:} Study autonomous multi-step editing and interface-probing agents against visual provenance detectors, paired with low-bandwidth abstention interfaces (\textit{Can Pixels Alone?}) and adaptive verification.
  \item \textbf{Provenance Governance \& Policy Alignment:} Bridge technical limits with AI regulation and evidentiary standards (\textit{AIES '26}; IP/legal outreach), translating statistical error bounds and fixed-FPR calibration into actionable compliance frameworks.
\end{itemize}
